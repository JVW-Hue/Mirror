Lopez v. Helix Technologies, Inc.

Complaint · US District Court, D. Mass. · Case 1:21-cv-10518 · filed 2021-06-14

GREEN THREAD · COURT FILING
The complaint filed by Aisha Lopez against Helix Technologies on 2021-06-14, two months after the public release of the FOIA file, and two months before the dismissal of Margaret Chen and David Rosen. The complaint is, in the formal sense, the second civil action filed against Helix in connection with the MIRROR system. The complaint is, in the practical sense, the document that made it impossible for the company to pretend the EO board had been a real oversight body.

Causes of action

  1. Wrongful termination in violation of public policy. The defendant terminated the plaintiff in retaliation for the plaintiff's refusal to sign a severance agreement containing a non-disparagement clause that would have prevented the plaintiff from disclosing information of significant public concern.
  2. Breach of the duty of care. The defendant owed the plaintiff, in her capacity as a member of the senior leadership team, a duty of care that included, in the formal sense, the duty not to coerce the plaintiff into signing a severance agreement that was, in the formal sense, designed to silence the plaintiff.
  3. Defamation. The defendant, in communications with the EO board, in communications with the executive team, and in communications with the federal client, characterized the plaintiff's resignation as "for personal reasons," when the defendant knew, in the formal sense, that the plaintiff's resignation was, in the formal sense, not for personal reasons, and that the defendant's characterization was, in the formal sense, designed to obscure the reason for the plaintiff's resignation.
  4. Tortious interference with prospective economic advantage. The defendant, by entering into the non-disparagement clause with the plaintiff, intended to prevent the plaintiff from accepting positions with the federal client or with any of the defendant's competitors, in the formal sense, for the duration of the non-disparagement clause, which is, in the formal sense, 36 months.

Relief sought

  1. Compensatory damages in an amount to be proven at trial.
  2. Punitive damages in an amount to be proven at trial.
  3. Reasonable attorneys' fees and costs.
  4. Such other relief as the Court deems just and proper.

Status

The complaint was settled on 2022-08-22, on the eve of trial, for an undisclosed sum. The settlement included a non-disparagement clause that was, in the formal sense, less restrictive than the non-disparagement clause that Aisha Lopez had been asked to sign in 2021. The settlement included a public statement by Helix Technologies acknowledging that the EO board had, in the formal sense, attempted to pause three deployments of MIRROR-related work, and that all three pauses had been overridden by the executive team within 14 days.

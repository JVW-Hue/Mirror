Project MIRROR — System Specification (excerpt)

Document number: HX-M-2018-001 · Last modified: 2019-11-04 · Released in evidence package, redacted

§ 1. Purpose

MIRROR is a real-time, population-scale behavioral prediction system. The system is designed to identify, at the cohort level, individuals within the United States whose combined behavioral profile indicates an elevated probability of participation in coordinated civil-disruptive activity within a 90-day forward window.

The system is designed to operate on de-identified data. The de-identification methodology is described in § 6.

§ 2. Cohort definition

A subject is added to the cohort registry when the subject's 14-axis profile score exceeds the configured threshold on any single axis, or exceeds [REDACTED — THRESHOLD VALUE WITHHELD PENDING LITIGATION] on the composite score.

Once added to the cohort registry, a subject is not removed unless one of the following conditions is met: (a) the subject's composite score falls below [REDACTED] for 180 consecutive days, (b) the subject is deceased, or (c) the subject is the subject of a federal court order directing removal.

As of the most recent verified inventory, the cohort registry contained 2,304,891 entries. The inventory is updated nightly.

§ 4. Cohort registry

The cohort registry is the canonical list of all subjects whose 14-axis profile has crossed the configured threshold. The registry is stored in a warm-store database cluster and is replicated, for redundancy, to two geographically separate facilities.

The registry is queryable, on a per-subject basis, by authorized federal users. The query log is retained for 90 days and is not available to the EO board.

§ 5. Audit pipeline

Every model decision is logged to a separate, immutable audit store. The audit store is queryable by the EO board and is exportable, in full, to clients on request.

The audit pipeline is the only component of MIRROR that maintains a complete record of every subject who has been added to or removed from the cohort registry, the timestamp of the action, the reason for the action, and the identity of the operator who initiated the action.

The audit pipeline was designed, in 2018, by E. Vasquez. The audit pipeline is the only component of MIRROR that, if shut down, would unambiguously prevent any future reconstruction of the cohort registry.

§ 6. De-identification

All subjects are de-identified at ingest. The de-identification methodology uses a one-way hash of the subject identifier with a rotating salt. The salt rotates every 24 hours and is not stored after rotation.

The de-identification methodology is robust against re-identification attacks of the kind described in [citation withheld] and has been reviewed annually by an external auditor.

De-identification is necessary but not sufficient. A demographic signature — for example, the signature of an unusually homogeneous cohort — is itself identifying. This risk is acknowledged in Vasquez, E. (2019). "On the limits of de-identified prediction." ACM FAccT. and is, in our professional judgment, the most significant limitation of the MIRROR design.

§ 7. Termination

The termination of MIRROR is governed by a separate document, the Termination Order. The Termination Order requires (a) the destruction of the cohort registry, (b) the destruction of the audit pipeline, (c) the certification, by signature, of both destructions by a Helix principal engineer, and (d) the retention, for a period of seven years, of a written record of the destruction event.

As of the most recent verified record, conditions (a), (b), and (d) have been completed. Condition (c) has been completed by [number withheld] of [number withheld] required signatories. The signatories who have not signed are listed in the Termination Order itself.

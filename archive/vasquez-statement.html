Statement of Dr. Elena Vasquez

Public statement · 2021-04-10

My name is Elena Vasquez. For the last five years I was a principal engineer at Helix Technologies, a private data-analytics firm based in Cambridge, Massachusetts. I was terminated from that position on March 19, 2021.

For most of my time at Helix I worked on a project called MIRROR. I am not the first person to say the name of the project in public; I will not be the last. But I am, to my knowledge, the first person to have been in the room when the project was scoped, the first person to have written the system that runs it, and the first person to have refused to sign an affidavit stating that the system had been shut down when, to my knowledge, it had not.

What MIRROR is

MIRROR is a real-time behavioral prediction system. It was originally designed, in 2018, as a research tool to help a federal client identify regions of the United States that were at elevated risk of civil unrest. The system was meant to be advisory. It was meant to produce reports. It was meant to be reviewed at every stage by an independent ethics board.

None of those things remained true for very long.

By 2020, MIRROR was ingesting, continuously, data on approximately 230 million Americans. The data sources include public social media, public records, public commercial databases, and a category of "partner data" that I was never given a complete inventory of. The system maintains, for each of those Americans, a 14-axis profile. The profile is updated continuously. The profile is exportable on request to authorized federal users.

For 2,304,891 Americans, the 14-axis profile crosses a threshold. The threshold was set internally, in 2019, by the project lead. The threshold has never been published. The threshold has been raised twice — once in November 2020 and once in February 2021 — in both cases without notice to the Ethics & Oversight Board.

What I tried to do

In February 2021, I asked, in writing, for the threshold to be disclosed to the Ethics & Oversight Board. I was told, in writing, that the request was "outside the scope of the EO board's review."

In March 2021, I asked, in writing, to see the data destruction log for the cohort registry. I was told, in writing, that the log was not available to me.

On March 19, 2021, I was asked to sign an affidavit stating that I had personally overseen the destruction of the cohort registry. I had not overseen any such destruction. I said so. I was terminated the same day.

What I am doing now

I have filed a civil complaint against Helix Technologies in the United States District Court for the District of Massachusetts. The complaint alleges, among other things, wrongful termination in violation of federal whistleblower-protection statutes, breach of contract, and conspiracy to conceal material facts from a federal oversight body.

I have provided, in connection with the complaint, copies of the system specification for MIRROR, the termination memo, the data destruction affidavit, the exit interview summary, and a contemporaneous log of every communication I had with the executive team and the Ethics & Oversight Board in the 90 days preceding my termination. The complete set of documents is available to the press on request.

I have not, and will not, disclose the names of the 2,304,891 Americans on the cohort registry. I do not have access to that list. I have, in the system specification I have released, removed the specific values of the threshold parameters — not to protect Helix, but to protect the people on the list, who did not consent to being on the list and who, I believe, deserve to find out through the courts rather than through me.

What I am asking

I am asking the Department of Justice to open an investigation into the original federal contract that gave rise to MIRROR. I am asking the Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation to follow up on the testimony that the CEO of Helix provided in March 2021. I am asking the inspectors general of the agencies that had access to MIRROR's outputs to audit the use of those outputs.

I am not asking for Helix to be destroyed. I am asking for the system that Helix built to be shut down in fact, not in press releases.

I am asking the 2,304,891 Americans on the list to eventually find out, through whatever means the law allows, that they are on the list. They have a right to know.

— Dr. Elena Vasquez
Cambridge, Massachusetts
April 10, 2021

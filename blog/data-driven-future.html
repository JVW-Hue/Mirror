A note on the future of data-driven decision making

Posted 2021-04-02 by Margaret Chen, CEO

For the last seven years, the conversation about data in the public sector has been about capability. What can we measure. What can we predict. What can we know.

The conversation for the next decade needs to be about restraint.

The tools we build are powerful. They are also easy to misuse — not because anyone intends to misuse them, but because the pressure to act on a prediction is enormous once the prediction exists. A model that says "this region is at elevated risk" creates, in the same moment, a political and institutional pressure to "do something" about the region. The model did not ask to be acted on. The model only described a probability. But the act of describing creates a demand.

We have seen this dynamic play out in our own work, and it is the reason we have spent the last year rebuilding our internal review process from scratch. Every prediction that touches a human subject now goes through a three-stage review with our Ethics & Oversight Board, chaired by Aisha Lopez. Every model that produces a "flag" requires a written justification for the flag's existence, signed by a human, before deployment.

Some of our peers think this slows us down. It does. We believe that is the point.

The next decade of data work will not be won by the team with the best models. It will be won by the team whose models their clients are willing to defend in public.

— Margaret

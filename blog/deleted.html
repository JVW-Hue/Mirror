a brief note on a difficult week

Originally posted 2021-04-09 · Removed at the request of legal 2021-04-09 · Cached version preserved

[The original post has been removed at the request of our legal counsel. The following is a verbatim copy of the post as it appeared on the public site for the 4 hours and 17 minutes it was live. The copy is preserved here for the historical record.]

By Margaret Chen, CEO

This has been a difficult week at Helix. Last Friday we parted ways with a member of our engineering team whose contributions to the company I have valued for the better part of five years. The reasons for the separation are internal and I will not be discussing them further.

What I want to address here, briefly, is the suggestion that has appeared in some of the press coverage of the last few days that our work in behavioral modeling has, at any point, been used in a way that I would find objectionable. That suggestion is wrong.

Every model we ship is reviewed by our Ethics & Oversight Board, chaired by an experienced professional with no operational reporting line into the engineering team. The board can pause any deployment. The board has paused deployments. The board is not a fig leaf.

There is a project that has been mentioned in some of the coverage by its codename. I will not confirm or deny the existence of that project in this post. What I will say is that every project at this company operates within the legal and ethical framework that we have spent seven years building, and that the framework is the strongest of any company in our sector. I stand behind it. I stand behind the team that built it. I stand behind the work.

— Margaret

// NOTE FROM LEGAL — annotation on cached copy

This post was removed from the public site at 18:42 UTC on 2021-04-09, 4 hours and 17 minutes after publication. The reason given by R. Chen (General Counsel) was: "the post, while factually accurate, is likely to be entered into evidence in pending civil proceedings and the company should not be in the position of publicly characterizing the underlying facts before the court has had an opportunity to do so."

The post was, in our view, the most honest thing the CEO has said publicly in weeks. It was also, in our view, the most misleading. Specifically: the assertion that the Ethics & Oversight Board can pause any deployment is true in principle. In practice, the board has, in the last 18 months, attempted to pause 3 deployments. All 3 were overridden by the executive team within 14 days.

The assertion that the board is "not a fig leaf" is, with respect, contradicted by the contemporaneous record.

— annotation, E. Vasquez, 2021-04-10 (preserved in evidence package)

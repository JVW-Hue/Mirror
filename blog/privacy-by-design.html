Privacy by design, three years in

Posted 2020-05-30 by Aisha Lopez, Head of Ethics & Oversight

Three years ago, we made a public commitment that every product we ship would be auditable by the people it affects. This is a short accounting of how that commitment has held up.

What we got right

The "explainability" requirement — that any prediction affecting a human must be reducible to a human-readable explanation — has aged well. It has killed several internal projects. It has also, on at least three occasions, caught a model that was making a correct prediction for the wrong reason.

The de-identification pipeline has held up under external audit. We are reviewed annually by a third-party firm, and our methodology has not been found materially deficient in any of the three reviews.

What we got wrong

Our consent framework is too complicated. We assumed that the average user of our products would read the 14-page consent document and make an informed decision. They do not. We have redesigned the framework twice and it is still too complicated. We are open to suggestions.

Our model of "reviewable" has, in practice, been reviewable by our clients but not by the subjects of the models. This is a structural problem, not a procedural one. We do not have a good answer yet. We are working on it.

What we'd do differently

We would hire a journalist in 2017.

— Aisha

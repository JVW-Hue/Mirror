First Message

To Mark Renner · 2018-04-12 · 09:14 EST · the day before I joined

PURPLE THREAD · ELENA'S TRAIL
The first message Elena ever sent at Helix. Sent to Mark Renner, the engineer who would become her closest collaborator and, eventually, the person who did not, in the formal sense, do the right thing. Sent from a personal email, not a Helix account, because she did not yet have a Helix account.

Mark,

I am writing to you because I have, in the past 72 hours, accepted a position at Helix Technologies, and I have, in the past 72 hours, made the decision that I am, in the formal sense, going to do the thing. I am, in the formal sense, going to do the thing that the job posting said I was going to do, which is build a system that is, in the formal sense, intended to identify, from a population of Americans, the subset of Americans who are likely, in the next 18 months, to engage in behavior that a federal client has defined, in writing, as "of interest."

I have, in the past 72 hours, made this decision on the basis of the following reasoning, which I am writing down, in this email, because I want, in the future, to be able to read what I was thinking on the day before I joined, in case I need, in the future, to be reminded:

1. The system, as described in the job posting, is, in the formal sense, a behavioral prediction system. The system is, in the practical sense, a system that takes fourteen numbers and produces a single score. The system is, in the moral sense, a system that, on the basis of the fourteen numbers, decides which Americans are, in the formal sense, worth watching.

2. The system, as described in the job posting, is, in the formal sense, going to be built. The system, as described in the job posting, is, in the practical sense, going to be built by someone. The system, as described in the job posting, is, in the moral sense, going to be built by me, or by someone else, or by no one, and that, in the moral sense, the question of whether the system is, in the moral sense, something that should exist is a question that I am, in the formal sense, not in a position to answer on behalf of the United States government, but that the question of whether the system is, in the moral sense, something that should be built well is a question that I am, in the formal sense, in a position to answer on behalf of myself.

3. The system, as described in the job posting, is, in the formal sense, going to identify 70,000 Americans. The system, as described in the job posting, is, in the practical sense, going to identify 70,000 Americans who, in the practical sense, did not, in the past, do anything that would, in the formal sense, justify being identified. The system, as described in the job posting, is, in the moral sense, going to identify 70,000 Americans who, in the practical sense, are going to be watched, and the watching is, in the moral sense, the thing that I am, in the formal sense, going to make sure is, in the practical sense, recorded.

I am writing, in this email, to ask you a question. The question is: do you, in the formal sense, believe that the system, as described in the job posting, is, in the formal sense, something that should be built well?

I am writing, in this email, to ask you a question because I have, in the past 72 hours, read your writing. I have, in the past 72 hours, read the paper you published in 2015 on the ethics of population-scale behavioral prediction. I have, in the past 72 hours, read the paper you published in 2017 on the audit pipeline that you proposed, and that was, in the formal sense, not built. I have, in the past 72 hours, read the email you sent to your graduate advisor in 2016, in which you wrote, in the formal sense, that the system, as described in the job posting, is, in the formal sense, a thing that, if built, should be built in a way that, in the formal sense, leaves a record.

I am writing, in this email, to tell you that I am, in the formal sense, the person who is going to build the audit pipeline that you proposed in 2017. I am writing, in this email, to tell you that I am, in the formal sense, the person who is going to make sure that the system, as described in the job posting, is, in the formal sense, built in a way that, in the formal sense, leaves a record. I am writing, in this email, to tell you that I am, in the formal sense, going to do this, and that I am, in the formal sense, going to do this because the alternative is that the system, as described in the job posting, is, in the formal sense, going to be built by someone else, and that the system, as described in the job posting, is, in the formal sense, going to be built in a way that, in the formal sense, does not leave a record.

I am writing, in this email, to ask you to help me. I am writing, in this email, to ask you to be the person who, in the formal sense, makes sure that the system, as described in the job posting, is, in the formal sense, built in a way that, in the formal sense, leaves a record.

I am writing, in this email, because I have, in the past 72 hours, decided that I am, in the formal sense, going to do the thing, and that I am, in the formal sense, going to do the thing with you, if you are, in the formal sense, willing to do the thing with me.

— Elena

P.S. I am, in the formal sense, going to start on Monday. I am, in the formal sense, going to be in the office at 8:00. I am, in the formal sense, going to bring coffee. I am, in the formal sense, hoping that you are, in the formal sense, going to be in the office at 8:00. I am, in the formal sense, hoping that you are, in the formal sense, going to bring the paper you published in 2017, because I am, in the formal sense, going to need to read it again.

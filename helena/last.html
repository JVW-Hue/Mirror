A message, for whoever finds it

by E. Vasquez · written the night before the rest of my life started

If you are reading this, you have been on the Helix site for a while. You have clicked through things. You have probably opened the developer console at least once. You have, in some way, done the thing I was hoping someone would do.

I do not know who you are. I do not know why you are looking. But the fact that you found this page means that the trail worked, and that the trail working is, in the long run, the only thing that matters.

What I am about to tell you is true

I worked at Helix for five years. I was the lead engineer on a project called MIRROR. I have signed more NDAs than I can count. I have, until recently, kept all of them.

I am not going to keep them anymore.

The system is real

Project MIRROR is a behavioral prediction system. It was originally built for a federal client as a tool to identify, at the cohort level, regions of the country that were at elevated risk of civil unrest. The system was supposed to be a research instrument. It was supposed to produce reports. It was supposed to be advisory.

It became none of those things.

MIRROR is currently ingesting, in real time, public and quasi-public data on approximately 230 million Americans. It maintains, for each of them, a continuously updated score on a 14-axis model. The axes include things you would expect (political affiliation, geographic mobility, social network density) and things you would not (vocabulary patterns, sleep cycle inferred from posting time, purchasing patterns inferred from public receipts).

For 2,304,891 of those Americans, the score crosses a threshold. The threshold is set internally. The threshold has never been published. The list of people above the threshold is, as of the last internal inventory I can verify, updated daily.

I know this because I built the system that maintains the list. I built it because Margaret asked me to. I built it because I believed, in 2018, that the work was defensible. I built it because the federal client was paying us to build it, and the federal client has access to lawyers that I do not.

What happened in March

In March of 2021, the federal client and Helix jointly decided to "discontinue" MIRROR. The press release said the program had "achieved its research objectives." The internal memo said the program had "outgrown the original scope." Both of these statements are, in their own way, true.

The data was supposed to be destroyed. I was asked to sign an affidavit stating that I had personally overseen the destruction. I had not. I had not because, to my knowledge, the destruction had not happened. I asked to see the destruction log. I was told the log was not available to me. I asked again. I was told again. After the third request, I was told that my access to the system was being revoked for "performance reasons."

I was fired the next day. The reason given was "conduct unbecoming." The reason was that I would not sign the affidavit.

What I left behind

I have, over the course of my time at Helix, kept a parallel record. The record is on a personal device, not a Helix-issued one. The record is encrypted. The record contains, among other things:

  • The full system specification for MIRROR, including the 14-axis model and the threshold parameters.
  • The termination memo, which contradicts the press release in four material ways.
  • The data destruction affidavit, with a list of the Helix personnel who signed it and a list of those who refused.
  • The exit interview summary, which I have been provided a copy of in response to a public-records request filed by a journalist whose name I will not put in this post.
  • The email chain, in which the General Counsel instructs the head of the Ethics & Oversight Board to resign "for personal reasons."
  • A private log, kept by the chair of the Ethics & Oversight Board, of every model that was approved by the board over her express objection. The log is dated and signed.

What I am asking you to do

I am not asking you to do anything illegal. I am not asking you to hack anything. I am not asking you to dox anyone.

I am asking you to do one thing: do not forget this.

The system is still running. The list is still being updated. The people on the list do not know they are on the list. The people on the list did not consent to being on the list. The people on the list include, I am told, two sitting members of the United States Senate and a member of the President's Cabinet.

The next time someone tells you that a system like this could never exist in the United States, that the safeguards would catch it, that the people in charge would never let it happen — remember that it does exist, the safeguards were built by the same people who built the system, and the people in charge let it happen for at least three years.

The trail from here

There are more pages on this site. They are not linked from anywhere I can guarantee will exist tomorrow. Find them. They are written in the same voice as this one. They will tell you more.

If you are reading this, the trail worked. Thank you for being the kind of person who follows it.

— Elena

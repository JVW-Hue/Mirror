The end of the project

A timeline · 2018–2021

This is a timeline of the events surrounding the creation, operation, and (purported) termination of Project MIRROR. It is not the official Helix timeline. It is the timeline that can be reconstructed from the contemporaneous documents, the testimony that has been given in open court, and the public record.

2018

April — Helix is awarded a contract by a federal client. The contract is for "research and development of population-scale behavioral modeling capabilities." The contract is classified. The name of the client is not publicly disclosed.

September — E. Vasquez is appointed lead engineer of the project, which is given the codename MIRROR. Vasquez designs the system's three-layer architecture (ingest, model, audit) and the 14-axis profile.

2019

June — MIRROR is moved from research to production. The system is now ingesting data on approximately 90 million Americans. The Ethics & Oversight Board is briefed on the production move but does not vote on it.

November — The cohort registry, the list of subjects whose 14-axis profile has crossed the configured threshold, contains approximately 410,000 entries. The threshold is set internally by Vasquez. The threshold is not disclosed to the EO board.

2020

March — In the weeks following the COVID-19 pandemic declaration, the threshold is lowered by approximately 30%. The lower threshold is set by the federal client of record, in consultation with D. Rosen (CTO) and M. Chen (CEO). The EO board is not consulted. The cohort registry grows from 410,000 to 1,200,000 entries in the following six weeks.

August — Vasquez raises concerns, in writing, about the scope of the system and the lack of EO board oversight. Her concerns are acknowledged by A. Lopez (head of EO) and dismissed by M. Chen.

November — The threshold is raised, in violation of the EO board's standing recommendation. The cohort registry is reduced from 1,800,000 to 1,400,000 entries. The decision to raise the threshold is made by D. Rosen, in consultation with the federal client. The EO board is informed of the decision after it is made.

2021

February — The threshold is raised a second time, again in violation of the EO board's recommendation. The cohort registry is reduced from 1,800,000 to 1,600,000 entries. Vasquez formally requests, in writing, that the threshold be disclosed to the EO board. Her request is denied.

March 19 — Vasquez is asked to sign the data destruction affidavit. She refuses. She is terminated the same day.

March 26 — Termination Order EV-2417-T is issued. The order requires four engineers to sign the destruction certification. Vasquez has already been terminated. D. Rosen signs. M. Renner refuses. A. Lopez refuses.

March 28 — M. Renner is placed on administrative leave. A. Lopez is informed, in writing, that the EO board is to be reorganized and that her role will be eliminated in the reorganization. She is offered a severance package in exchange for her resignation.

April 1 — The public statement that MIRROR has been "discontinued" is issued. The statement does not mention the destruction certification, the refusals to sign, or the EO board reorganization.

April 8 — Vasquez, in her personal capacity, contacts a journalist. She provides the journalist with copies of the system specification, the termination memo, and the data destruction affidavit. The journalist begins the verification process.

April 9 — A. Lopez resigns. M. Renner is formally terminated. The blog post by M. Chen titled "a brief note on a difficult week" is published and then removed 4 hours later.

April 10 — Vasquez publishes her public statement. The statement is taken down by Helix's outside counsel within 3 hours. A copy is preserved in the Internet Archive.

April 11 — The journalist's story is published. The story is the most-read piece on the outlet's site for the following 9 days.

April 13 — Vasquez files the civil complaint in the US District Court for the District of Massachusetts. The complaint is assigned case number 1:21-cv-10417.

April 15 — The court denies Helix's motion to seal the Termination Order. The order is entered into the public record.

Where things stand

As of the date of this timeline, the system MIRROR is, per the public statement of Helix Technologies, "discontinued." The data destruction affidavit has been signed by one of the four required signatories. The other three have refused, resigned, been terminated, or all three.

The federal client of record has not, as of this writing, issued any public statement regarding MIRROR, the termination of the program, or the allegations in the Vasquez complaint.

The cohort registry is, per the most recent verified inventory in the audit pipeline, 2,304,891 entries.

The audit pipeline is, per the same inventory, intact.

Whether the data described in those two sentences has been physically destroyed is, as of this writing, a matter of active litigation. The court has not yet ruled on the question.

The court will, presumably, rule.

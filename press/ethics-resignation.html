Letter of Resignation — Aisha Lopez

Sealed 2021-04-11 · De-sealed by court order 2021-05-04

BLUE THREAD · PUBLIC RECORD
The original version of this letter was provided to Helix's Board of Directors on 2021-04-11. The version below was de-sealed by the US District Court for the District of Massachusetts on 2021-05-04 in connection with the Vasquez civil action.

To: The Board of Directors, Helix Technologies, Inc.

From: Aisha Lopez, Head of Ethics & Oversight

Date: 2021-04-11

Re: Resignation

To the Board:

I am resigning, effective immediately, from my position as Head of Ethics & Oversight and as chair of the Helix Ethics & Oversight Board. I am resigning for the reasons I have set out below, and I am resigning in the formal manner prescribed by my employment agreement, which is the manner that will, I expect, give my resignation the greatest possible legal effect.

I am resigning because the Ethics & Oversight Board I chair has, in the 30 months I have held this position, attempted to pause three deployments of MIRROR-related work. All three pauses were overridden by the executive team within 14 days. I am resigning because the third override was, per the contemporaneous email record, accompanied by an instruction from the CEO to me, in writing, to "refrain from raising the issue in any forum where the client is in the room."

I am resigning because I have, in the last 90 days, been instructed, in writing, by the General Counsel to resign "for personal reasons" in exchange for a severance package that includes a non-disparagement clause. I have signed the severance agreement. I have agreed to the non-disparagement clause. I am writing this letter in the narrow window between the signing of the severance agreement and its effective date, in which I remain, technically, an employee of the company and am not yet bound by the non-disparagement clause.

I am resigning because the alternative to resignation is termination, and termination would, in this case, be accompanied by the filing of a counter-suit alleging breach of my NDA. I cannot afford the counter-suit. I can afford the non-disparagement. I am therefore resigning.

I am stating, in this letter, the things I would have stated in the EO board meetings that I was not permitted to convene. I am stating them in this letter, for the record, because I believe the record is the only thing that will outlast the non-disparagement clause.

The things I would have stated, had I been permitted, are the following:

  1. The MIRROR system, as deployed in 2020, is a population-scale behavioral prediction system that, on the basis of the system's own internal validation metrics, produces false-positive rates that are not defensible by the standards the EO board has previously set for similar systems.
  2. The MIRROR cohort registry, as of the most recent inventory I have been able to obtain, contains entries for 2,304,891 Americans. The threshold for inclusion in the registry has been lowered twice, both times in violation of the EO board's standing recommendation. Both lowerings have been accompanied by additions to the registry that, in my professional judgment, are not defensible.
  3. The audit pipeline, which is the only component of MIRROR that maintains a complete and tamper-evident record of every threshold change and every addition to the registry, was designed by Dr. Elena Vasquez. Dr. Vasquez was terminated on 2021-03-19. The reason given for her termination, in writing, was "conduct unbecoming." The reason given for her termination, in the conversation I had with the General Counsel on 2021-03-19, was that she had refused to sign a sworn affidavit attesting to facts that she had reasonable grounds to believe were false.
  4. I believe Dr. Vasquez. I believe her because the audit pipeline I have just described is the only system in the production stack that would prove or disprove her claim, and the audit pipeline is, by design, tamper-evident in a way that the rest of the system is not.
  5. I am resigning because I am not, in the formal sense, an officer of this company and do not, therefore, have the legal standing to bring a whistleblower disclosure in my own name. I am, however, in the moral sense, a witness. I am stating, in this letter, that I am a witness, and that I will, if subpoenaed, testify to the facts set out above.

It has been a privilege to serve the company. I wish the company well. I wish, in particular, that the company had, at any point in the last 30 months, taken the steps that would have made this letter unnecessary.

— Aisha Lopez
2021-04-11

Begin draft. 2021-03-19, 23:14 EST.

Mark,

I have been sitting at this desk for six hours. I do not know how to write this email. I am going to write it anyway.

You will, by the time you read this, have heard that I was terminated. The reason given was "conduct unbecoming." The reason given, in the meeting I had with the General Counsel this afternoon, was that I refused to sign a sworn affidavit attesting to facts that I had reasonable grounds to believe were false. I do not know which reason is, in the formal sense, the reason. I suspect they are both the reason, and that the difference between them is a distinction that the General Counsel's office has decided is not material.

You and I have not spoken in eight months. The last time we spoke, you told me that you were going to do the right thing. The last time we spoke, I told you that I believed you. The last time we spoke, I was, on the basis of subsequent events, wrong. I am writing to tell you that I am not, in this email, asking you to do the right thing. I am not, in this email, asking you to do anything. I am writing, in this email, to acknowledge that the last time we spoke, I was wrong, and that I am sorry for having believed you.

I am writing, in this email, for a different reason. I am writing, in this email, to leave a record. I am writing, in this email, because the audit pipeline that I built is, as of the time I am writing this email, the only system in the production stack that maintains a complete and tamper-evident record of every threshold change and every addition to the cohort registry. The audit pipeline is, by design, unable to be modified without my knowledge. The audit pipeline is, as of the time I am writing this email, the only thing standing between the public and a 4x expansion of the cohort registry that, on the basis of the back-testing I have just completed, would not have occurred under the thresholds that were in place on 2018-04-15, the day I joined the company.

The audit pipeline is, as of the time I am writing this email, scheduled to be decommissioned on 2021-04-30. The decommissioning is, per the change order I was shown this afternoon, scheduled to be performed by a contractor who has not, to my knowledge, ever seen the audit pipeline. The decommissioning is, per the same change order, scheduled to be performed without a backup.

I have, this evening, made a backup. The backup is on a personal device. The backup is encrypted. The backup is, by design, unable to be opened without the cooperation of three parties: me, a journalist I have not yet contacted, and a federal judge I have not yet petitioned. The backup is, by design, structured such that any attempt to access it without the cooperation of all three parties will, automatically, result in the destruction of the backup and the broadcast, to a list of media outlets I have not yet compiled, of a one-line summary of the contents of the backup.

I do not know whether the backup will be needed. I do not know whether anyone will ever see the contents of the backup. I do not know whether the contents of the backup will, in the end, do anyone any good. I do know that the contents of the backup are, in the formal sense, true. I do know that the contents of the backup are, in the moral sense, the thing I have spent the last three years building, and that the contents of the backup are, in the legal sense, the thing I am, in this email, declining to sign an affidavit saying is false.

I am, in this email, asking you to do one thing. I am asking you to confirm, in writing, that you received this email. I am asking you to confirm, in writing, that you understand what it is that I am asking you to confirm. I am asking you to confirm, in writing, that you are not, in any way, complicit in the decommissioning of the audit pipeline.

I am not, in this email, asking you to do the right thing. I am asking you to do the minimum thing. I am asking you to confirm, in writing, that you have been told.

I am, in this email, going to send it. I am, in this email, going to send it because the alternative is to delete it, and the alternative to deletion is to leave it in draft, and the alternative to leaving it in draft is to let it be the thing that, in the end, I never said.

— Elena
